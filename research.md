# Task 1: Research

[Home](./) · **1. Research** · [2. Design](design.md) · [3. Plan](plan.md) · [4. Present](present.md) · [Skills](skills.md) · [Practice](practice.md)

**Time:** 3 hours · **Marks:** 15 · **Internet:** allowed

## What you produce

- A **technical brief** of about 1,500 words that sets out your research and initial thinking
- A **reference list** of every source used
- Your **research notes**

## How it's marked

| AO | Marks | Top band looks like |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 Plan | 3 | Methodical and thorough. Covers **every** requirement in the brief |
| AO2a Knowledge | 3 | Core knowledge applied across technology, materials and initial ideas |
| AO2b Skills | 6 | A full range of skills used consistently. This is the biggest chunk |
| AO3 Sources | 3 | A **wide range** of sources, all fully referenced |

## Timing plan (3 hours)

| Time | Do this |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Read the brief. Build a **requirements table** (requirement, value, how I'll research it) |
| 0:15–2:00 | Research each area (about 20 min each, see below), taking notes and saving references as you go |
| 2:00–2:45 | Write up the technical brief under clear headings |
| 2:45–3:00 | Check references, spelling, word count and file names |

## What to research (adapt to the brief)

Using the sample brief (an illuminated, people-counting street bollard) as an example:

- **Materials:** strength, impact resistance, corrosion and weather resistance, cost, recyclability and sustainable sources. *Compare at least 3*, e.g. steel (galvanised), stainless steel, aluminium, recycled polymer, concrete core.
- **Technology:** sensor options (e.g. PIR, infrared beam-break, time-of-flight, camera). Compare accuracy, power use, cost and vandal resistance.
- **Power and lighting:** off-grid options (solar PV + battery), LED efficiency (lumens per watt), battery chemistry (e.g. LiFePO4), and energy budget calculations.
- **Form and aesthetics:** context (railway station), visibility, anti-vandal features and existing products.
- **Base and foundations:** root-fixed vs surface-mounted, concrete foundation sizes and installation time.
- **Standards and regulations:** vehicle security barriers (e.g. IAS 13 / ISO 22343, formerly PAS 68), IP ratings (BS EN 60529), relevant BS EN material standards, and WEEE for electronics.
- **Existing products:** look at 2–3 real bollards and note what works and what doesn't.

> **Remember:** in Tasks 2 and 3 you **won't have the internet**, only your Task 1 brief. Write down the **numbers** you'll need later: material properties, costs, component sizes, power ratings and installation times.

## Suggested technical brief structure

1. Introduction: the client, the problem and the key requirements (as a table)
2. Materials research, with a comparison table and justified shortlist
3. Technology research (sensors, lighting, power), with a comparison table
4. Form, aesthetics and vandal resistance
5. Foundations and installation
6. Standards, legislation and sustainability
7. Initial ideas: 2–3 directions and how each meets the brief
8. Conclusion: what I'll take forward into Design
9. References (Harvard style)

## Top-band tips

- Use **comparison tables** with scores or ratings against the brief requirements. They show AO2b skill and save words.
- **Say why each fact matters to the brief.** For example: "aluminium is lighter, **which reduces installation time during the overnight access window**".
- Use a **range of source types**: manufacturer datasheets, British Standards, government or industry bodies, textbooks and supplier price lists.
- Use **Harvard referencing**: Author/Org (Year) *Title*. Available at: URL (Accessed: date).

## Common mistakes

- Copying and pasting with no link to the brief
- Researching only materials and forgetting the tech, power, foundations or standards
- No numbers, which leaves you stuck in Task 2
- A reference list that's just bare URLs
